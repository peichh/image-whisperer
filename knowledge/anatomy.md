# Anatomy (for images with people)

Use only what the image needs. Realistic styles need more care; stylised styles follow their own proportions (see the style file).

## Proportions

- Adults are roughly 7-8 heads tall in figure-drawing convention; children have larger heads relative to the body (a 5-year-old is roughly 5-6 heads). Fashion illustration stretches to 9+ heads; do not use that for realistic photos.
- Arms hang with wrists near the top of the thigh; elbows near the waist.
- State age as a number or range ("about 70"), not just "old", so the model keeps proportions and skin consistent.

## Faces and expression

- Real faces are slightly asymmetric. Avoid "perfect symmetrical face" for realism.
- Name a specific expression ("a small closed-mouth smile", "eyebrows raised in surprise") instead of "beautiful expression".
- Eye direction: say where the person looks (at the camera, at the child, at the phone). Unstated gaze often comes out as a blank stare at the camera.
- Eyes: catchlights should match the light source (one window = one soft catchlight).

## Hands

- Hands fail most often. Reduce risk: give hands a clear simple task ("holding a mug with both hands", "hands in pockets", "resting on the table").
- Count hands: every hand gets at most one task, and in a selfie one hand is holding the phone.
- Thai/East Asian "ชูสองนิ้วจิ้มแก้ม": a V sign with the tips of the index and middle fingers pressed into the cheek, palm toward the camera.
- Avoid asking for complex finger gestures unless needed; if needed, describe one gesture exactly ("a thumbs-up with the right hand").
- Hands touching objects need contact: fingers wrap around, pressure on soft things.

## Body and pose

- Weight: name which leg carries weight, or the support (leaning on a wall, sitting on a step).
- Balance: the centre of mass sits over the support. Dynamic poses need a reason (running, jumping, dancing).
- Multiple people: give each person a position and an action. Avoid identical poses and faces unless intended ("twins").
- Camera height changes proportions: close and above = big head, short legs; chest height from a few metres = natural; hip height = taller, more heroic.
- Respect body diversity: keep the body type the user describes or implies; do not slim or idealise by default.

## Skin and hair (realistic)

- Natural skin has pores, fine lines, slight unevenness, and varies by age. Use "natural skin texture" rather than "flawless skin".
- Hair has flyaways and responds to wind and gravity; describe length and texture ("shoulder-length wavy black hair, a few loose strands").
- Thai and Southeast Asian subjects: describe skin tone, features and hair naturally; do not lighten skin unless the user asks.

## Ages and sensitive subjects

- Children: everyday, age-appropriate clothing and activities only.
- Elderly: keep wrinkles, posture and real age features; do not de-age unless asked.

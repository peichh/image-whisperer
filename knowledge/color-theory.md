# Colour

Use when colour is part of the request (brand, palette, mood) or when the scene needs a coherent palette.

## Practical rules

- Name 2-4 main colours. More becomes noise.
- Tie colours to objects ("a mustard-yellow scooter", "#0A4D68 navy background"). FLUX.2 and Gemini follow object-bound hex colours; other models may treat hex loosely, so add the colour name too.
- One dominant colour, one secondary, one accent (60/30/10 is a useful guide).
- Contrast for legibility: text and subject must contrast with their background.

## Palettes by mood

| Mood | Palette |
|---|---|
| calm, clean | white, soft grey, pale blue, light wood |
| warm, cosy | cream, terracotta, warm brown, amber light |
| fresh, healthy | white, leaf green, lemon yellow |
| premium | black or deep navy, gold or champagne accent, lots of space |
| playful | saturated primary or candy pastels, high contrast |
| nostalgic | faded warm tones, slightly lifted shadows, film grain |
| Thai traditional | deep red, gold, dark green, indigo (temple and textile colours) |

## Harmony

- Complementary (blue/orange) = energy; analogous (green/teal/blue) = calm; monochrome = elegant/minimal.
- Keep skin tones natural; heavy colour grading on people often looks artificial.

# Physical realism

Realism = everything obeys the same physical world. Add only the items that apply to the scene.

## Gravity and support

- Every object rests on something or is visibly held/hung. Name the support for important objects.
- Cloth drapes and folds under gravity; heavy fabric (denim, wool) folds in fewer, larger folds than silk.
- Liquids: level surface in still containers; pouring forms a continuous stream; splashes need a cause.

## Contact and shadows

- Contact shadows where objects touch surfaces (feet, cups, tyres). Mention them in close-ups and product shots: "a soft contact shadow under the bottle".
- Shadow direction and softness follow the main light (see `knowledge/lighting.md`).
- Reflections must show the environment (a chrome kettle reflects the kitchen; a puddle reflects the sky and signs).

## Interaction with environment

- Wet ground darkens and reflects; feet splash in water; wind moves hair, clothes, leaves and smoke the same way.
- People sitting compress cushions; hands pressing leave dents in soft materials.
- Heat: steam from hot food and drinks, heat shimmer on hot roads.
- Dust, wear, scratches and fingerprints on used objects. Brand-new studio products are clean; everyday objects are not.

## Scale and perspective

- Keep real sizes. Mention scale cues when size matters ("a cat the size of a bus" for intentional fantasy; "a small 10 cm figurine on a desk").
- One horizon line; parallel lines converge consistently.
- Wide lenses close to the subject enlarge near parts; long lenses compress distance.

## Motion

- Motion blur only on moving things for slow shutter looks; frozen action for sports flash/high shutter.
- Hair and clothes lag behind motion direction.

## Atmosphere

- Distant objects lose contrast and shift towards the sky colour (aerial perspective).
- Bangkok/tropical scenes: humid haze, strong overhead midday sun, quick evening dusk.
